What is financial consolidation software?

Financial consolidation software combines the accounts of several companies into one set of group reports: a consolidated profit and loss, balance sheet and cash flow. It sits on top of the accounting system each company already uses, so nobody changes how they keep their books.

The core jobs it automates:

  • Pulling the data from each entity's accounting system on a schedule, instead of exporting and pasting.
  • Mapping charts of accounts so "Office costs" in one company and "Premises expenses" in another land on the same group line.
  • Converting currencies for subsidiaries that report in different currencies.
  • Eliminating intercompany transactions such as management fees and loans between group companies, so revenue and balances aren't double counted.
  • Producing reports such as board packs, dashboards and budget-vs-actual comparisons for the whole group or any subset of it.

Who needs it (and who doesn't)

You'll likely get your money back quickly if:

  • You own or manage two or more companies, such as a holding company with trading subsidiaries, a franchise group or a property portfolio.
  • Month-end group reporting is built in spreadsheets and takes hours or days.
  • You have entities in more than one currency.
  • Investors, lenders or a board expect consolidated figures on a regular basis.
  • You're an accountant or bookkeeper producing management reports for clients with multiple entities.

You probably don't need it yet if you run a single company with straightforward reporting. Your accounting software's built-in reports will cover you. That said, tools like Joiin also offer single-entity reporting, which some businesses use for nicer dashboards and report packs.

How to choose: a 5-point checklist

  1. Integrations: does it connect directly to every accounting system your entities use?
  2. Multi-currency: does it handle translation automatically, with the rates you need?
  3. Eliminations: can it remove intercompany balances and transactions without manual journals?
  4. Pricing model: are you charged per entity, per user, or per feature, and what happens as you grow?
  5. Reporting output: can you produce the board pack or client report you need without reworking it in Excel?

The fastest way to test all five is to connect two or three of your real companies during a free trial and try to rebuild last month's group report.

Frequently asked questions

What is financial consolidation software?

It combines the accounts of several companies in a group into one set of reports, such as a consolidated P&L, balance sheet and cash flow. It pulls data from each entity's accounting system, maps the charts of accounts together, converts currencies, and removes intercompany transactions so group numbers aren't double counted.

When does a business need consolidation software?

Usually once you run two or more companies and spend real time each month building group reports in spreadsheets. Other signs are multiple currencies, intercompany loans or recharges, or investors and lenders asking for consolidated figures.

Is consolidation software a replacement for my accounting software?

No. It sits on top of your existing accounting software. Each company keeps its own books in Xero, QuickBooks or similar, and the consolidation tool reads that data to build group reports.
